Planning Wedding Transportation: A Timeline Checklist

Flying Limo Journal · 6 min read

← Back to the Journal

Transportation is one of the last things couples plan and one of the first things guests notice when it goes wrong. A getting-ready party stranded because a car showed up late, or a photographer waiting on a bride who's still ten minutes out — these are avoidable problems with a little lead time.

Three months out: figure out the headcount

Before you book anything, map out who actually needs a ride and when. That usually means the wedding party from the getting-ready location to the ceremony, the couple from ceremony to reception (often with a photo stop along the way), and sometimes a shuttle for out-of-town guests between the hotel block and the venue. Write it out as a simple list of legs, not just "we need a car" — it's the difference between booking the right vehicle and scrambling later.

One month out: lock in vehicles and timing

This is when to finalize exactly which vehicles you need and confirm pickup times with buffer built in. A good rule of thumb: whatever your photographer's timeline says, add 15 minutes of cushion to every transportation leg. Hair and makeup almost always run long, and a driver who's been told the real buffer will plan the route accordingly instead of cutting it close.

How many vehicles do you actually need?

  • Small wedding party (up to 6): One SUV or sedan is often enough if timed well.
  • Full wedding party: A Sprinter van keeps everyone together and photo-ready, rather than splitting the group and staggering arrivals.
  • Guest shuttle: If more than a handful of guests are staying at a hotel block without cars, a shuttle running on a loop saves you from fielding "how do we get there" texts all day.
  • The couple's car: Many couples book a separate, more private vehicle just for themselves between ceremony and reception — a few quiet minutes before the room fills up.
"Add 15 minutes to every leg. Hair and makeup always run long."

The week of: confirm details that are easy to forget

Reconfirm addresses (not just venue names — exact loading zones and entrances), a day-of contact number for the driver or dispatcher, and any special requests like a specific song playing when the couple gets in the car, or space reserved for a dress with a long train. Small details, but they're the ones that turn a good transportation plan into a genuinely smooth day.

Day-of: let the driver own the timeline

Once the day starts, hand the schedule to your driver or coordinator rather than managing it yourself. The best chauffeurs treat the printed timeline as a starting point and adjust in real time — leaving a few minutes early if hair and makeup is running ahead, or holding if the ceremony starts late. That's exactly the kind of flexibility a rideshare app can't offer.
